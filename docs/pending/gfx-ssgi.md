# gfx-ssgi — Screen Space Global Illumination and Screen Space Reflections

Goal: Cycles-like soft bounce light, colour bleeding and soft reflections in realtime with
rasterization only (no hardware ray tracing, no path tracing).

## Progress

- **Post-process Volume overrides** (`scene/screen_trace_overrides.rs`), off by default and left
  out of scene files while untouched:
  - *Screen Space Global Illumination*: Intensity (0–4), Radius (m), Thickness (m), Quality
    (Low / Medium / High = 1 / 2 / 4 rays × 8 / 12 / 16 steps per pixel per frame), Full
    Resolution (default half), Temporal Accumulation, Spatial Denoise, Lightmapped Surfaces.
  - *Screen Space Reflections*: Intensity (0–1), Max Roughness, Thickness, Max Distance, Edge
    Fade, Quality (40 / 64 / 96 Hi-Z iterations), Full Resolution (default on), Temporal
    Stabilization.
  - Resolved into `PostSettings::{ssgi, ssr}` (`render/screen_trace/settings.rs`).
- **Renderer** (`render/screen_trace/`): see Decisions for the technique. Per render, after the
  shared depth + normal prepass: Hi-Z pyramid, SSR trace / temporal / roughness blur, SSGI
  trace / temporal / 3 à-trous iterations, compose into one packed texel per pixel (f16 GI rgb +
  visibility, f16 SSR rgb + confidence) in a storage buffer at mesh group binding 17. After the
  opaque part of the main pass the resolved HDR colour is copied into a per-view colour history
  with a 6-level box mip chain; the next render reprojects into it.
- **Lit shaders** (`screen_trace/shaders/screen_trace.wgsl`, included by mesh, terrain, grass,
  water and custom shaders): `shade` computes the probe / sky reflection into `reflection`, then
  `screen_gi_apply` and `screen_reflection_apply` (the only change to the combine point).
- **Inspector**: two new Post-process Volume sections (`post_volume_trace.rs`) with Denoising and
  Baked Lighting sub-headers.
- **Scene view**: Effects dropdown ▸ *Screen Space GI & Reflections* (also follows Post
  Processing, like every volume effect).
- **Frame Debugger**: events *Screen Space Reflections* (Hi-Z + trace + temporal + blur),
  *Screen Space Global Illumination* (trace + temporal + denoise), *Compose Screen Trace
  Texels*, and *Screen Space Lighting History* (copy + mips). None of them exist while both
  effects are off.
- **Sample**: `Scenes/ScreenSpaceGIDemo.scene` generated by `sample_ssgi.rs` (white room, red
  and green walls, skylight with the sun on the floor by the red wall, white sphere and blocks,
  glossy floor; volume with SSGI, SSR and AO). Screenshots `docs/screenshots/gfx/ssgi_demo_*.png`
  (off / SSGI / SSR / both), regenerated by the ignored `write_ssgi_screenshots`.
- **Tests**: shaders validate with naga (`screen_trace::tests`); GPU headless
  (`screen_trace::gpu_tests`): red wall bleeds onto the floor and SSGI never darkens past AO
  (with AO on as well, no double darkening); mirror floor shows the cube with SSR; temporal
  accumulation settles (frame-to-frame change falls); with both off no pass is recorded and the
  image equals a renderer that never ran them; demo check (both wall colours bleed). Ignored
  benchmark `cost_at_1080p`.
- `Presets/Stormy Sky.preset` regenerated (`write_sample_presets`): its excluded-field list
  gained the two new volume overrides (values unchanged).

## Decisions

1. **SSGI = short world-space ray marches, cosine-weighted, not a horizon / visibility-bitmask
   integral.** Each pixel marches 1–4 cosine-distributed rays (R2 sequence rotated per pixel
   and per frame) with quadratically spaced steps over Radius. A sample behind the depth
   buffer by less than Thickness is a hit and brings back last frame's lit colour at that
   pixel. Why: colour bleeding wants the *radiance* of what the ray meets, and a march gets it
   directly; the bitmask method (Therrien et al. 2023) shines at occlusion with thickness
   heuristics, which GTAO already covers here. The march is also easy to reason about (each
   ray is one Monte Carlo sample of the cosine integral) and its noise is what the temporal +
   à-trous passes are built for. Hi-Z was kept for SSR, where rays are long; GI rays are short
   (a few metres, 8–16 steps) so a plain march costs about the same.
2. **Units match the ambient term**: the lit shader's ambient is `ambient_radiance × albedo`, so
   the bounce adds `mean(hit radiance) × albedo` (cosine-weighted average of incoming radiance);
   no extra π factors.
3. **Misses fall back to the existing ambient, never to black.** Rays that leave the screen, find
   nothing, hit a point the previous frame didn't see, or hit before any lit history exists,
   count as "visible": that share keeps the ambient / light probe / reflection probe diffuse the
   shader already computes. The bounce multiplies the ambient by the visible share and adds the
   hit light.
4. **No double darkening with AO**: with SSAO on, AO alone darkens the ambient and the traced
   visibility is ignored (hits still add light). Without SSAO the traced visibility acts as a
   (noisier) AO, so the image is never darker than with AO alone (tested).
5. **Lightmapped surfaces skip SSGI by default** (their lightmap already contains baked bounce
   light; adding screen-space bounce would count it twice). *Lightmapped Surfaces* turns it on
   for scenes where dynamic objects should tint baked ones. Light-probe-lit objects take SSGI
   (probes are the off-screen fallback).
6. **Infinite bounces through feedback**: the history is the lit image, which already contains
   last frame's SSGI, so light converges to multiple bounces over frames. Hit radiance is clamped
   (32) against fireflies and Intensity is capped at 4.
7. **Temporal accumulation**: exponential history, blend `1/n` with n capped at 24 frames, per
   pixel history length and linear depth stored beside it; bilinear reprojection taps rejected
   when last frame's depth there doesn't match (disocclusion) → n restarts at 1. Camera cuts
   (> 5 m or > ~32° turn) reset. Moving lights lag by up to ~24 frames (accepted; Temporal off
   removes it).
8. **Spatial denoise**: 3 à-trous iterations (spacing 1, 2, 4; B3 5×5) weighted by plane distance
   and normal agreement, at GI resolution; then a bilateral (depth + normal) upsample to full
   resolution in the compose pass. Spacing is a pipeline-overridable constant (three pipelines).
9. **SSR = Hi-Z traced mirror rays + roughness blur**, not stochastic GGX rays: the trace runs in
   screen space (pixels + device depth, affine along the projected ray) through a closest-depth
   pyramid (min of 2×2, 3×3 on odd edges), climbing a level after each empty cell and descending
   where the ray may pass behind the closest surface; thickness tested at level 0. Roughness:
   the hit is read from a colour mip matched to the cone (roughness × distance), then an
   edge-aware 12-tap Vogel disc blur sized by roughness², premultiplied by confidence. Why: one
   ray per pixel and a stable result without heavy temporal denoising; the blur approximates
   glossy lobes well enough up to the Max Roughness fade.
10. **SSR fades** by roughness (to Max Roughness), at screen edges (Edge Fade, both at the hit and
    at its reprojected history position), towards Max Distance and for back-facing hits; the
    rest keeps the reflection probe / sky reflection (`mix` in the lit shader with the same
    Fresnel × roughness weight as the sky reflection).
11. **SSR temporal**: history reprojected with the surface's motion and clamped to the 3×3
    neighbourhood of this frame's trace, weight 0.85.
12. **Both read last frame's lit colour**, copied after opaque geometry (so gizmos, transparent
    meshes and particles are not bounced or reflected): the main pass splits there as for the
    scene copy, without the depth resolve when only SSGI / SSR need it.
13. **Smoothness for SSR comes from the prepass**: the normal target's alpha (unused before) now
    holds `1 + material smoothness` (the per-draw value; maps are not sampled in the prepass).
14. **One storage buffer instead of textures for the lit shaders**: the mesh / terrain pipelines
    are at the 16 sampled-texture limit (terrain already uses all 16), storage buffers had room
    (6 of 8). The buffer holds packed half-float pairs, encoded / decoded by hand in WGSL
    (`half_bits` / `screen_trace_half`): `pack2x16float` / `unpack2x16float` need naga's
    `SHADER_FLOAT16_IN_FLOAT32` capability, which the editor's shader validation (custom shaders,
    Shader Graph) and some adapters lack. The shader tests validate with default capabilities.
15. **Zero cost when off**: no textures, no passes, no copy, no main-pass split; the lit shaders
    read a 48-byte zero header (one uniform branch). Resources are per view and per effect (only
    the enabled effect's images are allocated; rebuilt when resolution or effects change).

## Cost

Whole-frame GPU time (timestamp queries, `cost_at_1080p`, test room at 1920×1080, this
machine's GPU, Medium quality):

| Config | GPU ms | Added |
|---|---|---|
| Off | 0.83 | — |
| SSGI (half res, temporal + denoise) | 2.90 | +2.1 |
| SSR (full res) | 3.92 | +3.1 |
| SSGI + SSR | 5.18 | +4.4 |

Both include the shared depth + normal prepass (~0.2 ms here) and the colour history copy +
mips. SSR at half resolution or Low quality is the first knob when it is too expensive; an
obvious next optimisation is a tile classification pass that skips sky and too-rough tiles
before the trace.

Headless results: floor beside the red wall (sRGB) off (177, 184, 196) → SSGI (217, 185, 193);
mean image brightness off 163.9, AO only 163.6, SSGI 167.2; mirrored cube in a metal floor
off (130, 150, 182) sky → SSR (168, 21, 17) red; temporal frame-to-frame change 1.24 → 0.18
(frames 1→2 vs 10→11). Demo: floor by the red wall (63, 70, 94) → (85, 77, 100), by the green
wall (49, 75, 94) → (52, 86, 97).

## Left / ideas

- Quality ▸ tiers for SSGI / SSR (like Ambient Occlusion) are not wired; the volume's Quality
  field is the only control.
- The prepass reads the material's scalar smoothness only (smoothness maps don't vary SSR
  roughness per pixel; the lit shader still uses the map for the reflection weight).
- No per-object motion vectors: reprojection assumes static geometry (moving objects ghost
  briefly in the GI history; the depth rejection limits it).
- SSR rays towards the camera are shortened to stay in front of the near plane; reflections of
  things behind the camera fall back to probes / sky as in every screen-space method.
