# Meadow Wildflowers (Misty Engine sample assets)

Four wildflowers for terrains, made entirely by Misty Engine's own tools: every texture was
generated by `tools/flower_textures.py` (procedural, no photos, no third-party or downloaded
assets). They are used by the `MeadowDemo` scene and are free to use in your own games (see
`LICENSE.txt`).

| Flower | Colour | Files |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knapweed (Centaurea) | purple | `Knapweed*.png` |
| Poppy (Papaver rhoeas) | red | `Poppy*.png` |
| Oxeye Daisy (Leucanthemum vulgare) | white | `Daisy*.png` |
| Buttercup (Ranunculus acris) | yellow | `Buttercup*.png` |

Each flower is one atlas, 256 × 1024, in four maps:

| Texture | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `<Flower>.png` | Albedo, sRGB, alpha = cut-out. |
| `<Flower>Normal.png` | Tangent-space normal map (OpenGL: green up). |
| `<Flower>Roughness.png` | Roughness (grayscale, 1 = rough; buttercups are glossy). |
| `<Flower>Translucency.png` | How much sunlight passes through (petals glow, stems less). |

Layout: the top 256 × 256 square is the flower head seen from directly above (centred); the
256 × 768 below it is the whole plant from the side, its stem entering at the bottom centre and
its main flower head centred 8 % below the top of that region. The engine draws each flower
as two crossed plant cards and a head card lying nearly flat at the top of the stem.

## Using them

1. Select a Terrain with Grass & Details, open the **Paint Texture** tab (🖌) in the Inspector.
2. Open **Flowers** and tick **Enabled**: the four wildflowers are added as flower kinds (and
   their textures copied into `Assets/Flowers` when the project has none).
3. Set how dense the patches are, how much of the grass they cover, their size, a few lone
   flowers between them and how much they gather at the meadow's edges (around trees and
   where the grass thins out). Each kind has its own amount, height and head size.
4. **Paint Flowers** turns the brush into a flower mask (Shift + click thins flowers out, for
   paths and lawns); **Clear Paint** forgets it.

Flowers sway with the grass's wind, cast small shadows and thin out (each growing a little
bigger) in the distance, so patches keep their colour.

## Making the textures again

    python tools/flower_textures.py SampleProject/Assets/Flowers

(NumPy, SciPy and Pillow; deterministic, `--preview` also writes review sheets). The script
documents its layout and conventions.
